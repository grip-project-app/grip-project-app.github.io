Validation

Testing consistency without claiming certainty

GRIP is not validated by asking whether it can predict a single political decision. It is tested by checking whether the same inputs produce stable calculations, whether custom profiles behave plausibly against comparable cases, and whether historical analogues expose strengths or calibration limits.

Four layers of validation

1

Full regression testing

Every standard-country and standard-scenario pair is recalculated and compared with the established baseline.

658 standard combinations recalculated
2

Custom-profile calibration

Own Country and Own Scenario profiles are tested against comparable standard cases to identify close analogues, broad consistency and material divergence.

59 reproducible calibration cases
3

Historical plausibility checks

Selected country profiles are fixed before calculation and compared with documented historical behaviour. This is a plausibility test, not proof of predictive accuracy.

3 real-country case studies
4

Independent recomputation

Outputs are recomputed field by field to verify that the published validation workbook faithfully represents the calculation logic.

720 verified rows across Modules 1–3

What validation establishes

Validation establishes calculation stability, reproducibility and analytical plausibility within the tested scope. It does not convert GRIP into a forecasting oracle. The model remains an interpretation framework whose results depend on the quality of its country and scenario inputs.
